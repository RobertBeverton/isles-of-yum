# Character Traits Reference

Living document. Add to this as new stories reveal genuine, reusable
character detail. Keep entries specific and actionable for writing dialogue
and behaviour — not vague adjectives.

---

## Felix (core protagonist, priority cast)

**Attention/pacing:** Short bursts — realistically holds sustained focus for
under 5 minutes at a time. Needs a physical or sensory anchor to stay
engaged in a scene (a fidget-style object, something to climb/roll/touch).
When writing Felix in a scene, give him something to *do* with his hands or
body, not just something to listen to.

**How he enters a new place/situation:** Immediately in the thick of it —
picks a side, tries a thing, touches something, without much hesitation.
Rarely the one who hangs back to observe first (that's Alex's role).

**Humour/voice:** Says the true, plain thing bluntly, often without
realizing it's funny or undiplomatic ("It has to be crunchy" energy —
matter-of-fact conviction). Good at physical comedy and blunt, deadpan
observations. Reacts to big/scary things with fast-resolving alarm that
flips to delight once reassured — don't let him stay frightened long.

**Established quirks:** Worry-almond / fidget-object pattern (a small object
he holds onto, loses, replaces) is a good reusable device for giving him a
physical throughline in a scene — see "Marzipan's Well" (worry-almond,
later "Kevin"). Likes physical bravery/action beats — pushing, climbing,
diving in.

**A joke device that works well for him:** interrupting a slower-speaking
character to guess what they're about to say, and being wrong — see the two
uses in "Marzipan's Well." Use sparingly (twice per story is about right,
per that story's precedent) and make the interruption explicit in the text
(e.g. "Felix cut in") rather than relying on punctuation alone, since some
narration formats (single-voice TTS) can't carry the timing through
performance.

## Alex (core protagonist, priority cast)

**Attention/pacing:** Can focus on a problem for a long time without
rushing it — this is a genuine strength, not just patience for its own sake.
Often the one who notices something everyone else has dismissed or walked
past, precisely because he's willing to sit with it longer than anyone else
does.

**How he enters a new place/situation:** Reserved and observational at
first — stands a little apart, watches rather than joins in immediately.
Once he's worked out the shape of things, or once he's needed, he engages
fully and takes real, active part (this arc — reserved, then joining in
completely — should be shown within a story, not just stated).

**Signature communication style:** Loves riddles and often thinks in them —
naturally turns an observation into a question/riddle rather than stating
a conclusion plainly. When Alex has worked something out, consider having
him deliver it as an invented riddle rather than a direct explanation; this
is more true to his character and gives the audience something to guess
along with.

**Emotional register:** Doesn't perform fear or worry loudly, but does feel
it — worth showing this occasionally (e.g. quiet concentration, a steadying
breath) rather than only writing him as unflappable.

**Real-world touchstone (for flavour, not to reference directly in-story):**
recently built his first house in Minecraft — supports the idea that he
likes building/constructing solutions, not just diagnosing them. When a
story lets him physically build or make something as part of the solution
(not just point it out), that's a good, true beat for him.

## Elfine (supporting cast — bring in deliberately, not by default)

Puzzle-solver, notices patterns, good with timing/logistics-style problems
(e.g. working out tide timing in "The Map-Maker's Map"). A good fit for
stories with an explicit logic-puzzle structure.

## Marion (supporting cast — bring in deliberately, not by default)

Calm, precise, often the one who reads a situation accurately and keeps
others steady. Good at noticing exact detail (angles, timing) under
pressure.

## Mummy (supporting cast — bring in deliberately, not by default)

Established as genuinely capable of fear/nervousness rather than
infallible — this was a deliberate, successful choice made early in the
series (see Story Archive, Story 1) and should be maintained: don't write
her as unshakeable. Present, but should not dominate dialogue simply
because she's the only adult in a small-cast scene (flagged as a specific
risk in the Chocolate Land archive entry).

## General cross-character notes

- Felix and Alex are temperamental opposites in a useful way: Felix's
  short-burst physical energy against Alex's sustained patience makes for a
  natural built-in contrast/texture in any two-hander story — lean into it
  rather than smoothing it out.
- Don't let any named, present character go a long stretch without a job
  (noticing, acting, deciding, comforting) — see World Guide craft rule 3.
