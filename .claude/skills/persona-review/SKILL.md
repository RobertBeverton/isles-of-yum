# Persona Review

A structured way of reviewing a story from more than one angle, so problems
get caught before the story is considered finished — not a single "is this
good" pass, but several specific, different lenses, each looking for
something the others might miss.

## Baseline personas — always run these two

### 🧒 Five-year-old listener
Ask: is it fun? Is it clear? Is anything confusing, or scary in the wrong
way? This lens cares about immediate engagement and comprehension, not
craft — it should catch things like: a joke that won't land for a young
audience, a plot point that's hard to follow, tension that's genuinely
distressing rather than exciting.

### ✏️ Editor
Ask: does the structure work? Is there tension-type variety (no two
adjacent beats sharing the same flavour)? Does it follow the villain rules
(see World Guide craft rule 1)? Is character balance right (does everyone
present have a job)? Is jeopardy calibrated correctly (resolved fast,
teamwork-driven, ends in relief not dread, per World Guide craft rule 2)?

## Situational personas — run when relevant, skip when not

### 🛋️ The tired parent reading aloud
Relevant whenever the story will be read aloud live by a parent, which is
most of the time for this project. Ask: is this easy to voice cold, without
rehearsing first? Are different characters easy to distinguish without
heavy acting? Is the length right for an actual bedtime slot? Does any joke
or beat rely on the reader nailing comic timing on a first read — and if
so, does the *text itself* carry enough of that timing (dashes, explicit
"X cut in," a beat description) rather than depending on performance?

### 🎧 Narration format / TTS constraints
Relevant whenever the story will be turned into narrated audio (see
`audio-narration-prep` skill). Ask: does this rely on vocal contrast to
land a joke or tonal shift? If narration will be single-voice, that
contrast won't come through — check that interruptions, asides, and pivots
are carried by explicit text instead. If narration will be multi-voice
(ElevenLabs Text to Dialogue or similar), check that dialogue is cleanly
attributable to a single speaker per turn — avoid constructions where two
characters' lines are woven into one sentence in a way that's hard to split
into turns.

### Additional personas, as the format demands
Invent and run further personas when a story's specific format calls for a
check the above don't cover — e.g. a persona checking that an interactive
"choose together" story's decision points are genuinely fair and guessable,
not just one obviously-right answer and one obviously-wrong one.

## What to do at outline stage vs. draft stage

- **At outline stage**, personas are checking the *shape* of the story —
  structure, pacing plan, whether the idea is strong enough, whether the
  cast and locations are right. Nothing is prose yet, so don't nitpick
  sentence-level things.
- **At draft stage**, personas re-run against actual prose — this is where
  issues that only exist in real sentences show up (a joke's exact timing,
  whether an emotional beat has room to land, whether a paragraph reads
  awkwardly aloud).

## Cross-reviewing the notes (required step, not optional)

Once all relevant personas have given their notes, review the notes against
each other before recommending or making any fix:
- If two or more personas flag the same underlying issue from different
  angles, treat that as a strong signal it's a real problem worth fixing —
  not a coincidence to shrug off.
- If personas' notes point at the same passage but suggest different fixes,
  look for a single edit that satisfies all of them, rather than layering
  separate fixes that might conflict or over-correct.
- Not everything flagged needs fixing. Distinguish a real gap (something
  that would genuinely confuse, bore, or fail to land for the actual
  audience) from something minor and net-neutral. State which is which
  when presenting findings, rather than treating every note as equally
  urgent.

## Output format

Present each persona's findings clearly labelled (use the emoji headers
above for quick scanning), then a short "reviewing these together" section
that does the cross-check explicitly, then a clear final recommendation:
fix these specific things, or no fixes needed.
